## The owner's answers for Act VIII (Oct 31, 2026)
- **The Supers: open nods.** Clear, parody-style homages to famous heroes are allowed (no trademarked names, logos or exact
  costumes). There are still **no real cities, agencies or flags** anywhere in the act.
- **The escalation is a plain clock**, not the Wanted Level. The run climbs from gangs to police to the army to the
  heroes on fixed times, like the other acts. There is no "cut the comms".
- **The third shared rule is Civilians**, replacing the Wanted Level. Owner: "Civilians appearing at the beginning is
  awesome, but we're not sparing them. Let them drop XP to reward killing them."
  - Fleeing civilians fill the streets at the start of a run.
  - The army kills them like any enemy, and each one drops XP.
  - There is no sparing, and no penalty for killing them.
- **The maps are city blocks.** Owner: "let's try to make the maps on this stage a series of blocks and rectangular
  streets with alleys rather than our normal". The act's arenas are a grid of rectangular building blocks with streets
  and narrow alleys between them, instead of scattered obstacles.
- The act's three shared rules are therefore **Armour, Supers and Civilians**, with the escalation built into the
  timeline.

## Act VIII: The Last Line
The living fight back. Behind the Court's veil were the last great cities of the living: millions of people, lit all night, with guns. The necromancer's dead walk into the streets, and the city answers the way a modern city does, in rising order: the gangs who own the blocks, then the police, then the army, and at the end its superheroes. The look is asphalt and concrete, sodium-orange streetlights, neon, olive drab and hazard yellow, with the heroes in bright primary colours that pop off everything else. Police lights pulse slowly (no fast strobes: a photosensitivity rule for the whole act).

### Shared rules
1. Wanted Level (your escalation, as the headline). A five-star threat meter, like a crime game's, decides who the city sends. The clock raises it slowly, and kills of the city's responders and destroyed police cars and checkpoints raise it faster; each star brings the next tier into the spawn table:
| Stars | Who comes | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| ★ | Gangs | Thugs, gang gunners, drive-by cars |
| ★★ | Police | Riot lines, squad cars, a helicopter spotlight that marks the necromancer |
| ★★★ | SWAT and the Guard | Breachers with flashbangs, armoured trucks, medics |
| ★★★★ | The Army | Rifle squads, machine-gun nests, tanks, attack helicopters, artillery |
| ★★★★★ | The Heroes | A Super joins the fight every 90 s |
The player can push it down: radio masts on the map can be destroyed ("cut the comms") for −1 star and a Soul Ash bounty, so every run has a choice between farming the higher-paying tiers and keeping the heat low. Each stage caps the stars (Stage 22 at 3, Stage 23 at 4, Stage 24 starts at 4 and reaches 5), so the act itself also climbs from criminals to heroes. One path, WantedLevel, read by the spawn director; the stars sit on the HUD in place of Dread.
2. Armour. The city has vehicles: cars, trucks, APCs and tanks drive in straight lines (headlights show the lane 1 s ahead), run over every summon in the lane, then turn and come again. A destroyed vehicle becomes a burning wreck, a new obstacle for the rest of the run (and it explodes a few seconds later, hurting both sides). Over a run the open streets fill with wrecks and the arena changes shape. One path, Convoy, using the existing obstacle and push-out code.
3. Supers. Named superheroes, each with one power and one weakness, both shown on their plate ("Fast. Can't turn."). They are the act's elites and its bosses' allies, and they reward reading the weakness more than raw damage. One kit base, SuperKit, like CovenantKit and EmpireKit.

The Supers (Stage 24, and five stars anywhere): Blitz (a speedster who dashes through the ring; can't turn mid-dash, so a Bone Wall stops him cold), Atlas (a strongman who throws wrecks; a long wind-up), Aurora (a flying energy blaster, figure style; falls out of the sky when stunned), Nightjar (a gadget vigilante who grapples summons out of the ring; must land to strike).
Reckoning, Martial Law: at 0:00 the stars break past five: every tier spawns at once, artillery walks across the arena, and every Super the run has met returns.

### Stage 22: Skid Row
The docks and back streets of the city, where the dead first come up out of the sewers.


Zones: the Alleys, the Night Market, the Overpass, the Precinct.

Radio masts on rooftops and the Precinct's antenna: the first stage teaches "cut the comms".

Miniboss, Big Sal and the Armoured Limo, an enforcer who rams the ring while his crew shoots from the windows.

Boss, Don Marrow, the Kingpin: the Bounty (he puts a price on the necromancer's head: gang spawns double while his bounty board is up; break the board to end it), the Call (he phones the police on you: the Wanted Level jumps to three stars for the rest of the fight), the Last Stand (behind the limo's armour with a gold-plated minigun that sweeps the arena).

### Stage 23: Fort Kessler
A military base on the edge of the city, the army's last line against the dead.


Zones: the Checkpoints, the Minefield, the Airfield, the Command Bunker.

Minefields: blinking mines that blow up under anyone, both armies; luring the riflemen over them is the counterplay, and the ring has to flow around them (one ground path, like Act VI's pits).

Miniboss, Mammoth, a super-heavy tank with two turrets.

Boss, General Vance Harrow, "the Hammer": phase 1, he commands from a command vehicle and calls carpet-bombing lines across the arena; phase 2, a tank column and his own power armour; phase 3, Broken Arrow: he orders a strike on his own position, a 30 s countdown on screen. Kill him in time and it is called off; fail and the blast hits everything on screen (his army included), leaving the necromancer badly hurt and most of the army gone.

### Stage 24: Liberty Heights (the finale)
The heart of the city: the plaza, the skyline and the Heroes' Tower.


Zones: the Plaza, the Rooftops, the Tower Lobby, the Watchtower.

The Supers come in turn: Blitz, Atlas, Aurora and Nightjar each lead one zone as its miniboss.

Boss, Paragon, the Last Hero: the strongest man alive. The Hero (flight, heat-vision beams that cut lines through the ring, a punch that sends a summon across the arena), Hope (every Super still alive in the run flies in to his side: kill them earlier and this phase is short), No Restraint (he stops holding back: the sky turns white, and he dives from orbit on telegraphed craters that crack the arena).

When he falls, the world of the living has no one left to defend it.

Finale summon class: the Fallen Hero, a raised Super who flies around the ring and slams into packs (evolution Black Paragon).

Counter-passives (Act VIII only): Kevlar Bones (summons take less ranged damage), Off the Grid (the Wanted Level rises 30% slower).

Achievements: Radio Silence (cut every mast on Skid Row), Broken Arrow (kill the General before the countdown ends), No More Heroes (beat Paragon with no Super at his side; opens the Act VIII epilogue).
